# Designing Your Life — topic slate

Bill Burnett and Dave Evans. Source for the next set of reels.

**The count: 18 reels that stand alone. 12 of them are strong.**

Below, tier 1 is where I would start. Tier 2 works but each is narrower. Tier 3 I
would not make, and the reasons are listed so you can overrule me.

Rights policy is unchanged. Our own words, no quoted passages, title and authors
named as attribution only.

---

## What changes from the Dalton set

You were right that a couple of those only made sense in sequence. That was a
choice I made and it cost you: a viewer landing on part four cold has no idea
what the first three set up.

Every reel below is built to be someone's first. Each one names a problem the
viewer already has, explains one mechanism, and hands over one thing to do. No
callbacks, no "in the last video", no part numbers in the captions. If two reels
happen to be watched back to back they reinforce each other, but neither needs
the other.

Hooks are written to land inside three seconds and to work with the sound off,
because the hook is also the first burned caption.

## Tier 1 — the twelve I would make

| # | Working title | The one mechanism | Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Follow your passion is bad advice | Most people have no single pre-existing passion. Passion is produced by getting good at something, not found before it | Follow your passion is the worst advice you have ever been given |
| 2 | Some problems are gravity | A problem you cannot act on is not a problem, it is a circumstance. Energy spent fighting it is gone | Some of your problems are not problems. They are gravity |
| 3 | You are solving the wrong problem | Finding the right problem comes before solving it. Most stuck searches are well-executed answers to the wrong question | You have been solving the wrong problem for two years |
| 4 | Being good at it is the trap | Competence is not a reason to stay. The better you get at work you dislike, the more expensive leaving becomes | Being good at your job is the trap |
| 5 | Four gauges, not one | Health, work, play and love each get a reading. People diagnose work when the empty tank is play or love | You think you need a new job. You might need a new Saturday |
| 6 | The two week log | Track what you were doing and how engaged you were. The pattern in the log names the work to look for | Keep this log for two weeks and it tells you what job to look for |
| 7 | You have three lives in you | Write three separate five year plans: the current path, the path if it vanished tomorrow, the path if money and image did not matter | You do not have one life plan. You have three |
| 8 | Ask for the story, not the job | Asking for a job makes people defensive. Asking how they got where they are makes them generous | Stop asking for a job. Ask for their story |
| 9 | Nobody is qualified for that job | A posting is a committee wish list, not a specification. The person hired never matched it either | Nobody is qualified for that job. Including whoever gets it |
| 10 | Try the job before you take it | You can test a career for a day or a week. Most people commit years to something they never sampled | You can try a career for one day before you spend five years on it |
| 11 | Your degree is not your destiny | Most graduates do not work in the field of their major, which makes the degree a starting point and not a sentence | Your degree decided nothing |
| 12 | It is not too late | Too late is a belief, not a fact, and it is the single most expensive one a job seeker carries | It is not too late. That is a belief, not a fact |

## Tier 2 — six that work, but narrower

| # | Working title | The one mechanism | Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | More options, less happy | Gather options, narrow hard, choose, then stop reopening the decision | More options are making you miserable |
| 14 | Sort your failures into three piles | Screwups, weaknesses, growth. Only the third pile is worth carrying forward | Write down your failures, then sort them into three piles |
| 15 | You cannot think your way to a career | Clarity is produced by action, not before it. Trying beats deliberating | You cannot think your way to a career |
| 16 | Three maps, one job title | Mind map from your own high points and combine branches. Roles surface that you did not know were roles | Three mind maps will find you a job title you have never heard of |
| 17 | Redesign the job you have | Most roles have more slack than people use. Reshaping this one can beat leaving it | You might not need a new job. You might need to redesign this one |
| 18 | Two questions, simpler search | Write what work is for and what life is for, then check whether they agree | Answer two questions and your job search gets much simpler |

## Tier 3 — what I would leave out, and why

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| Build a life design team | Sound advice, but it lands in the same territory as our Dalton reels on contacting people. It would read as repetition |
| Wayfinding by clues | It is the same mechanism as the two week log and as "you cannot think your way to a career". Three reels, one idea |
| The front door job search is the worst method | This is our episode 2 almost exactly. Making it again would put two near-identical reels on one account |
| Reframing as a skill | It is the connective tissue of the book rather than a standalone idea. It works better inside reels 1, 2 and 12 than as its own |

## How I would sequence the first five

Not as an arc. Just the order I would post them, strongest hook first while the
account is small and every reel is somebody's first impression.

1. Follow your passion is bad advice
2. It is not too late
3. Nobody is qualified for that job
4. You think you need a new job. You might need a new Saturday
5. Being good at your job is the trap

Numbers 1 and 3 are the two most likely to be argued with in the comments, which
is the point.
