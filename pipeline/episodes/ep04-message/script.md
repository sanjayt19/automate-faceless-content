# The Message That Actually Gets Answered

Source: *The 2-Hour Job Search* by Steve Dalton. Own words only, no quoted passages.

## Narration

Your message is not read, it is pattern matched. Inboxes overflow with slop, polite, fluent, and forgettable. Recruiters learned the shape, they delete on sight. The old template is dead, but Dalton's rule holds. Keep it short and plain, six lines, that is all. No pitch, no resume, line one, who you are. Two lines, not ten, line two proves you looked. Name something only readers know, not their mission statement. Something from their work, that one line does everything. It cannot be mass produced, line three makes the ask. Fifteen minutes, not more, never ask for a job. They cannot give one, ask about the work. That they can answer, then make yes easy. Offer two slots only, close it, send it. No attachment on message one, attachments read as pitches. Most will not reply, that is normal, expected. Follow up once, one week, shorter than the first. Then stop, move on. Volume does not work, ten specific notes win, because specific cannot be faked. That is the whole signal, write fewer, write like yourself.
