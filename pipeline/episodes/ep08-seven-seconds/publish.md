## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** Your Resume Gets Seven Seconds #shorts

**Description:**
Recruiters were tracked with eye cameras during resume screening. The first pass
averages about seven seconds, and the path their eyes take is almost identical
every time: name, current title, current company, dates, previous title, dates,
education. Then the decision is made.

Your bullet points are not read on that pass. Neither is your summary paragraph.
That is not laziness, it is arithmetic, and it means the top third of the page is
carrying the entire load.

Most people bury that top third. A skills wall, a career objective, a paragraph
about being results driven. None of it survives seven seconds.

Three fixes. Match your title line to the words the posting actually uses, not
the internal title your company invented. Put one real number next to it, because
scale beats adjectives every time. Delete the objective, which nobody has ever
read.

Then test it. Cover everything below the first third of the page, look at what is
left for seven seconds, and ask what you learned about this person. If the answer
is nothing, the top needs rewriting.

This is not a design problem. Fonts never failed anyone. Buried information fails
everyone.

Summary in our own words, from published eye-tracking research on recruiter
screening behaviour.
